Session 9 is done. I decoded the area header sub-tables (`10:8300`, read by `AreaSubTable` `30:C7A5`) and worked out what `$6D` means. The task item also asked for three more things, which I didn't do: I moved them into a new task. None of this has been checked in an emulator.

**What I found**
- **Header layout:** most area headers hold 5 pointers (sub-tables 0–4), with the data right after. Only 10 areas have a 6th pointer: `6A 6D 6E 8A 9F A2 A5 AE AF B4`.
- **Sub-table 0** (`AreaLoadMap` `31:E628`) holds:
  - the area's music id, passed to `PlaySound`;
  - the width and height in screens (`$62FC`/`$62FD`);
  - two flag bytes, `$62FE` and `$62FF` (what they do is still unknown);
  - a grid of screen ids copied into `$6300` (up to 8 wide × 16 high). `$80` means "no screen".
- **Sub-table 1** (`31:E6B9`) holds the background palette ids (`$07E0-$07E2`; `$07E3` is always `$7F`), two values `$67`/`$69`, and the background CHR banks R0/R1.
- **Sub-table 2 and `$6D`:** `$6D` is the index of a 4-byte entry in this table, and that entry sets the starting camera position (`$34-$37`, X lo/hi and Y lo/hi). `$6D`=`$FF` means no entry, and `$2x` keeps the current camera position.
- **Sub-table 4:** pairs of (flag, screen). When the player enters a screen, it is marked in a visited-screens bitmap (`$62F0-$62FB`, 8×12, probably for a map display). Screens on this list also set a saved flag in `$64C0`, so they stay marked visited after the player leaves the area and comes back.
- **Sub-table 5** (`31:E573`, only the 10 areas above): links from a position in one area to a position in another. They are probably for falling down a pit into a lower area.
